# Code of Conduct

This project is part of [OpenRAC](https://openrac.dev), an unofficial,
community-run home for the *Ratchet & Clank* decompilations. Many of us are
here for the same reason: to understand a game we love, and to hand that
understanding on in a form other people can build on.

This Code of Conduct says what we expect of each other. It is adapted from
the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org), with the
parts that fit this project's work made specific.

## Our pledge

We pledge to make participation in this community a harassment-free
experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible
disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression,
level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal
appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming,
diverse, inclusive and healthy community.

## What we expect

### About the work

A decompilation is a big job: thousands of functions, each of which has to
come out as exactly the same bytes as the original. Nobody finishes that
alone, and nobody is competing for a prize. When another project figures out
a function, it is not a point lost for us — it is one more piece of the game
that all of us now understand.

So:

- **Disagree kindly.** Projects make different choices, and that is fine.
  Argue about the code, never about the person.
- **Credit what you reuse.** If you learned something from another project,
  a file or a function, say so in the source and in the commit, and name its
  licence. This project keeps [THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md)
  for exactly that.
- **Share what you find.** A symbol name, a structure, a compiler quirk: it
  helps everyone, whichever release or approach they work on.
- **Welcome newcomers.** Every one of us started out not knowing how any of
  this works. Reviewing, documenting function behaviour and improving the
  tooling all help, not just writing matches.
- **Be patient with people's work.** A pull request is someone's evening.
  Review it when you can, say why something does not match, and expect the
  same back.

### About the game

This project exists because of a game that someone owns and loves. Two rules
protect it, and they are not negotiable:

- **Never upload or link game data.** No disc images, executables, level
  overlays, dumps, extracted assets or other copyrighted material, in an
  issue, a pull request, the Discord, or anywhere else. This includes files
  that a tool generated from your own copy of the disc. Describe what you
  see in words instead; a screenshot of a disassembly or a note about a
  function's behaviour is welcome, retail bytes are not.
- **Never use leaked or NDA material.** Sony's SDK source, samples and
  headers are never used here, not even as a reference to check a match
  against, and neither is anything leaked or under NDA. If a function looks
  like SDK sample code, it is decoded from the assembly. This is the rule
  the whole project stands on ([AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md), "Sources"; the
  [sourcing policy](https://github.com/OpenRAC/OpenRAC/blob/master/docs/policy/SOURCING.md)
  upstream).

Breaking the first rule is also the fastest way to get this project into
trouble with the rights holder, and it is not worth it for any function.

### In general

Examples of behaviour that contributes to a positive environment include:

- demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people;
- being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints and experiences;
- giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback;
- accepting responsibility and apologising to those affected by our
  mistakes, and learning from the experience;
- focusing on what is best not just for us as individuals, but for the
  overall community.

Examples of unacceptable behaviour include:

- the use of sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or
  advances of any kind;
- trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political
  attacks;
- public or private harassment;
- publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email
  address, without their explicit permission;
- deliberately posting game data in the project's spaces;
- other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

## Responsibilities of maintainers

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these standards of
acceptable behaviour, and for taking appropriate and fair corrective action
in response to any behaviour they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive
or harmful.

Maintainers have the right and responsibility to remove, edit or reject
comments, commits, code, wiki edits, issues and other contributions that are
not aligned to this Code of Conduct, and will communicate the reasons for
moderation decisions when appropriate.

A maintainer who does not follow this Code of Conduct is subject to the same
process as anyone else, and is judged by the other maintainers.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all project spaces — this repository,
the other OpenRAC repositories, the OpenRAC website and its issue tracker —
and also when an individual is officially representing the community in
public spaces.

It covers the OpenRAC Discord, which is open to everyone and shared by all
the projects. Examples of representing our community include using an
official email address, posting via an official social media account, or
acting as an appointed representative at an online or offline event.

## Reporting

If you see or experience behaviour that breaks this Code of Conduct, or have
any other concern, please report it to the maintainers of this repository,
[Lynder063](https://github.com/Lynder063) and
[Veradictus](https://github.com/Veradictus), **privately**:

- by direct message on the [OpenRAC Discord](https://discord.gg/Sfd2B54PDG),
  which is open to everyone; or
- by a private message on GitHub.

Please **do not open a public issue** for a conduct report, and do not
mention it in a pull request. A report you make in good faith will be
handled as confidentially as we can make it.

When reporting, it helps to include what happened, when and where, who was
involved, and any link or copy of the message. If you are not sure whether
something breaks this Code of Conduct, ask us anyway.

## Enforcement

All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly. All
maintainers are obligated to respect the privacy and security of the reporter
of any incident.

Maintainers will follow the Community Impact Guidelines below in determining
the consequences for any action they deem in violation of this Code of
Conduct.

### 1. Correction

**Community impact**: use of inappropriate language or other behaviour
deemed unprofessional or unwelcome in the community.

**Consequence**: a private, written warning from the maintainers, providing
clarity around the nature of the violation and an explanation of why the
behaviour was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.

### 2. Warning

**Community impact**: a violation through a single incident or series of
actions.

**Consequence**: a warning with consequences for continued behaviour. No
interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with
those enforcing this Code of Conduct, for a specified period of time. This
includes avoiding interactions in community spaces as well as external
channels like social media. Violating these terms may lead to a temporary or
permanent ban.

### 3. Temporary ban

**Community impact**: a serious violation of community standards, including
sustained inappropriate behaviour.

**Consequence**: a temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public
communication with the community for a specified period of time. No public or
private interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited
interaction with those enforcing this Code of Conduct, is allowed during
this period. Violating these terms may lead to a permanent ban.

### 4. Permanent ban

**Community impact**: demonstrating a pattern of violation of community
standards, including sustained inappropriate behaviour, harassment of an
individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of classes of individuals.

**Consequence**: a permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within
the community.

## AI-assisted contributions

Tools that write code with a model's help are welcome here; several of the
OpenRAC projects use them. Two things follow from that:

- **You are responsible for what you contribute.** A pull request written
  with an AI is yours, the same as one written by hand: it has to match
  retail, it has to follow the project's rules, and you have to be able to
  explain it.
- **The rules do not relax for generated code.** It is still your
  contribution, and the sourcing rules above apply to it exactly as they
  apply to anything else.
